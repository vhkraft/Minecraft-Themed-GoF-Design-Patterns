--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
RECOMMENDED LEARNING ORDER FOR BEHAVIORAL DESIGN PATTERNS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

LEVEL 1: CORE ACTION & EVENT NOTIFICATION
These patterns introduce the fundamental concepts of swapping algorithms dynamically at runtime 
and decoupling objects through event-driven observation.

1. Strategy (COMBAT ATTACK STRATEGY)
- Why first: It is the most intuitive behavioral pattern. It teaches you how to encapsulate 
     interchangeable algorithms (e.g., melee vs. ranged attack strategies) behind a common interface, 
     replacing massive conditional statements.

2. Observer (REDSTONE OBSERVER BLOCK Crop harvest)
- Why second: Introduces event-driven programming and loose coupling. Instead of polling, objects 
     automatically notify registered listeners when their state changes (like a redstone observer block 
     detecting crop growth to trigger a harvest action).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
LEVEL 2: EXECUTION SKELETONS & STATE MANAGEMENT
These patterns focus on standardizing algorithmic steps via inheritance and managing internal object 
states without bloated conditional logic.

3. Template Method (POTION BREWING WORKFLOW TEMPLATE)
- Why third: It builds naturally on Strategy by defining an algorithmic skeleton in a superclass 
     while delegating specific variable steps to subclasses (similar to standardizing the potion brewing workflow).

4. State (WARDEN AGGRESSION STATE MACHINE)
- Why fourth: Shows how an object can drastically alter its behavior when its internal state changes, 
     cleanly replacing complex nested switch-case blocks (such as the Warden shifting through sniffing, 
     roaring, and attacking states).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
LEVEL 3: REQUEST ENCAPSULATION & COLLECTION TRAVERSAL
These patterns turn operations into first-party objects and provide uniform ways to traverse 
underlying data structures.

5. Command (ARMOR STAND POSE ADJUSTER STICK)
- Why fifth: Encapsulates actions as standalone objects, decoupling the sender from the executor 
     to enable queuing, logging, and undo/redo capabilities (like the armor stand pose adjuster stick recording commands).

6. Iterator (VILLAGER TRADE ROLL CYCLER)
- Why sixth: Provides a standard, uniform way to sequentially traverse elements of an aggregate collection 
     without exposing its underlying internal representation (like cycling through available villager trade rolls).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
LEVEL 4: PIPELINE ROUTING & CENTRAL COORDINATION
These patterns handle multi-object interactions by passing requests down sequential handler chains 
or routing communications through a central hub.

7. Chain of Responsibility (Hopper Transfer Pipeline Filters)
- Why seventh: Teaches you how to pass requests down a dynamic pipeline of independent handlers 
     until one takes ownership, completely decoupling senders from receivers (like hopper pipeline filters sorting items).

8. Mediator (VILLAGE RAID BELL ALARM)
- Why eighth: Simplifies complex many-to-many object communications by routing all interactions 
     through a single centralized controller object rather than allowing tangled direct references 
     (like the village raid bell alarm coordinating villagers and iron golems).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
LEVEL 5: STATE SNAPSHOTS, EXTERNAL INSPECTION & LANGUAGE PARSING
The most advanced tier, focusing on saving object states, executing external operations across trees, 
and parsing formal grammars.

9. Memento (BED CHECKPOINT & NETHER DEATH RESPAWN)
- Why ninth: Focuses on capturing and safely externalizing an object's internal state so it can be 
     restored later without breaking encapsulation barriers (like bed checkpoints and respawn data snapshots).

10. Visitor (TOOL DURABILITY & REPAIR INSPECTOR)
- Why tenth: Reverses traditional object-oriented encapsulation by allowing you to add brand-new 
     operations across diverse object structures externally without modifying their classes (like the tool durability & repair inspector visiting items).

11. Interpreter (CHAT COMMAND INTERPRETER)
- Why eleventh: The summit of behavioral patterns. It defines a formal grammar representation and 
     builds an Abstract Syntax Tree (AST) to evaluate expressions and commands dynamically (like parsing chat commands).
