--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
RECOMMENDED LEARNING ORDER FOR STRUCTURAL DESIGN PATTERNS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

LEVEL 1: THE CORE WRAPPERS (SINGLE INTERFACE TRANSLATORS)
These patterns introduce the fundamental concept of wrapping an object to change how it is 
accessed or used.

1. Adapter (Piglin Barter System)
- Why first: It is the most intuitive structural pattern. You have one target interface, and 
     you wrap a single incompatible object (like a Piglin bartering system) to make it fit, 
     establishing the basic idea of composition over rewriting.

2. Facade (Crafting Recipe Book)
- Why second: Like the Adapter, it wraps things, but instead of translating an interface, 
     it simplifies multiple complex subsystems into one clean entry point without complex polymorphism.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
LEVEL 2: CONTROLLED & DYNAMIC WRAPPERS
Once you understand basic wrapping, these patterns add behavioral control or runtime stacking.

3. Proxy (Faction Vault)
- Why third: Uses the exact same structural shape as an Adapter or Facade, but introduces 
     access control or lazy initialization, wrapping an object to protect it or delay loading.

4. Decorator (Diamond Sword Enchantments)
- Why fourth: Takes wrapping to the next level by allowing you to stack multiple wrappers 
     dynamically at runtime (e.g., wrapping a sword in Sharpness, then wrapping in Fire Aspect).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
LEVEL 3: DECOUPLED & HIERARCHICAL STRUCTURES
These patterns move away from simple 1-to-1 wrapping into managing independent axes and tree collections.

5. Bridge (Tool Material Bridge)
- Why fifth: Shifts your mindset from wrapping to decoupling. Instead of an inheritance tree 
     exploding in size, you split an abstraction from its implementation so they vary independently.

6. Composite (Shulker Box)
- Why sixth: Introduces recursion and tree structures, teaching you how to treat individual 
     items (leaves) and container groups (composites) through the exact same unified interface.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
LEVEL 4: ADVANCED PERFORMANCE OPTIMIZATION
The most advanced structural pattern focused on deep memory management.

7. Flyweight (Oak Leaf Blocks)
- Why last: The most complex structural pattern because it requires splitting state into 
     two categories (intrinsic shared data vs. extrinsic coordinates) and managing them through 
     a caching factory.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
